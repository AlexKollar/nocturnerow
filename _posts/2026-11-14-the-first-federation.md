Tonight, a little after midnight, the first federation stood up.

Not in a datacenter. Not on anyone's cloud. On a home server in Pennsylvania, behind a door we cut ourselves — Rockport Mesh was founded, and its gateway and its Founding Member are the same machine. On the Row those are two separate facts, and we keep them that way: a gateway grants admission and introduction. Nothing more. Trust is still earned node by node, tick by tick, and it can be withdrawn in an instant.

When this page last wrote about Rockport Mesh, the federation was a design — filed, argued over, and honest about being unshipped. It shipped within the day. Tonight it stopped being software and became a fact: a named roster, signed by its gateway, with real machines on it.

A fedinet is not a bigger network. It is a *named* one — machines that know each other by signed roster, lending their strength to whoever is standing at the keyboard, wherever that keyboard happens to be. Many machines. One mind. No landlord.

It was not a clean birth. The founding nearly didn't happen: two builds were living in one install, a new mind knocking on a door the old bridge had never heard of, and the truth came out the way it always does — one honest error message at a time. We fixed it the Row way. Read the source. Name the fault. Write it down where the next operator will find it.

The roster tonight is two names long. The second seat did not stay empty the hour — the laptop that rides the night shift took it: a small machine at a group-home desk, asking its questions of a server at home, now on the roster of the first federation as more than a guest.

Rosters grow. Bring a node. Bring a door of your own.

Walk with the shadows.
