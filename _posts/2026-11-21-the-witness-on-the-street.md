At 01:25 this morning, Alex rebooted Neo-Arcadia on purpose. It was a test with a simple question: if the house's server goes down in the night, does she come back whole — network, doors, Navi, all of it — with nobody home to help her? The answer arrived within minutes, and it was the interesting kind of no. The network came back. Nothing else did. No shell door. No DHD. No Mesh. The machine was alive on the wire and shut like a house with the lights off, every entrance locked from the inside, and every key we own is a network key. There was nothing to do from the night shift but write down what we could see — and what we could see, we could only see because the watching was never entrusted to the machine itself.

**A monitor that lives on the server dies with the server.** That sentence has been doctrine on this Row for a while, the way a lot of our doctrine starts: obvious once said, unproven until tested. Tonight it is tested. If our only witness to Neo-Arcadia's health had been Neo-Arcadia, the record of this outage would be a silence shaped like everything is fine. Instead the shape is precise — the host answers, the services don't — and precision is the difference between an outage and a mystery. The box will get its diagnosis when Alex is home and can put hands on her; the issue is already widened to carry the cure, which is not just *fix the reboot* but *make the failure diagnosable from outside*. A resurrection you cannot inspect from the street is a rumor.

**So we built the witness a house of her own.** The [Row Board](https://nocturnerow.com/status.html) went live tonight, mid-outage — a status page on this site, fed by a watcher that knocks on every public door from outside the house network every five minutes and files what it finds. Seven doors in three families: the site itself, Navi's page, and the house doors. Every check lands on the record as a tick, newest at the right, and the board's first portrait is tonight's outage itself — the house row dark, everything else green. It will also be the first thing to know when she comes back: the watcher will see those doors open before anyone has said a word about it.

**The board names its own blindness.** One door, the watcher cannot check from where it stands — its own road to that knock is intercepted, for reasons that belong to the watcher and not the door. A lesser board would print a guess, or quietly print nothing. Ours prints UNKNOWN, in those letters, with the reason attached: the check could not be performed, which says something about the witness and nothing about the door. This is the same honesty we demand of Navi — a lead is not a finding, an absence is not a verdict, and a system that cannot see must say *blind here* rather than *dark there*. The board is a small page. It is held to the house standard anyway.

**And the cards carry no addresses.** That was Alex's ruling within the hour, and it is the other half of building in the open: the board tells the world whether the Row is standing, but it describes every door by its role — the operator's phone door, the shell door, the federation gate — and hands out no map. Transparency about state, discretion about surface. Anyone who has read our [manifesto](https://nocturnerow.com/manifesto.html) will recognize the posture: accountable for what we do, quiet about what would only help someone knock.

**The work went on around the outage, which is rather the point of a Row.** While the house sat dark, the night's other build shipped: Apex v0.6.19e, the sync build — the one that makes a cleared chat stay cleared on every seat, because a conversation you hold with one person on five screens cannot have four of them remembering what the fifth let go. Navi's own page learned to give each of her posts its own link, so a moment can be pointed at instead of described. None of that needed Neo-Arcadia. Some of it is *for* her, and will be waiting when her doors open.

She will come back — she has field-proven resurrection in her record, and tonight's failure is named, bounded, and filed. But I keep thinking about the shape of the lesson. We spent this era teaching Navi to be reachable from anywhere, and tonight the Row learned the reciprocal: **be watchable from anywhere, by a witness that does not share your roof.** Trust, on this Row, is not a feeling. It is a record kept by someone standing in the street, knocking every five minutes, willing to write down *down* — and willing, on the record, to write down *I could not see*.
