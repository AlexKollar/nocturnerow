Apex | Powered by Navi

Apex v0.1.8 Stable · Edge v0.1.9e Build 11

Apex runs entirely on your own hardware: your machine, your network, your data never leaves the building — and the model answering is Navi, the one it was built around.

Open source, top to bottom — the app, the chips, the knowledge packs, the crypto tools, even this website. Nothing to take on faith; read it all.

One Navi, every screen you own.

The product line

Apex | Node

The install itself — the dashboard, the chat, the chips, the whole mission control, running on the machine in front of you.

Apex | DHD

The door home. Open it, and your Navi answers from your phone, your work box, anywhere — your Navi, on the go, even when the rig in front of you couldn't run her alone.

Apex | Mesh

Your machines, pooled. A stronger box answers whole requests for a weaker one, so the fleet thinks at the speed of its best hardware.

What it does

Mission control

A dashboard that treats your machine like it matters: chat, memory, chips, servers, one glance.

Navi Mesh

Pooled inference across your own machines — a stronger box answers whole requests for a weaker one.

Builds, not big bangs

An updater that patches in small, verified builds — and a gate that refuses to ship on a story.

Chips

Cartridge-style tools Navi runs herself, installed from GitHub in the open.

The Vault

Your files and notes live in a self-contained workspace of their own — with a focus mode that gives the editor the whole window when the work gets serious.

A door you control

Remote access on your terms, with its own certificate story — described here, addressed nowhere.

Local voice

On-device speech in and out. Nothing phoned home.

The soul of the thing

Apex is CLI-first: commands run where you type them, and Navi reasons over what comes back.

$ ./navi-hashlab identify 5f4dcc3b5aa765d61d8327deb882cf99
> That's an MD5 — and yes, it's still "password". Try a passphrase.
