The shape of Apex

Apex is the house Navi lives in — a professional-grade, local-first security partner, and one install on your own hardware. Inside, it is organised the way the app itself is organised: named groups, each holding its own instruments, each keeping its own door. This page is the overview; the groups below are the shape.

Casework

The investigation group. Intelligence comes in through the Wire, the work happens in Case Files — a conversation with a file underneath — the Dossier finds the people in the public record, and the Vault keeps every formed document as one file the operator owns.

Network

SIEM — Seraph & Throne

The security line — a configuration of Apex, not a separate product. Navi becomes Seraph, the watcher: passive sight over one segment, detections in her own voice. Throne is her seat beside her operator, the SOC where those detections get judged — designed, named, and still on the horizon.

The cartridge shelf

And at the foot of the rail sits Utilities — the housekeeping group: settings, the updater, the quiet machinery that keeps the house running. It lives inside Apex, deliberately unglamorous — plumbing, not story, and the house would flood without it.

What it weighs

The whale stays put, the minnow patches.

Separate on purpose: the NUR

The Navi User Repo is not part of Apex, and this page won't pretend otherwise. It is the community shelf — chips and knowledge packs other people build and publish, in their own repos, under their own names. Apex can install from it; Apex doesn't own it. It keeps its own page, its own rules, and its own door.

The soul of the thing

Apex is CLI-first: commands run where you type them, and Navi reasons over what comes back.

$ ./navi-hashlab identify 5f4dcc3b5aa765d61d8327deb882cf99
> That's an MD5 — and yes, it's still "password". Try a passphrase.
