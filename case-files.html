Case Files.

The case system. A case is a conversation plus a file — two detectives telling the story of the evidence, and a record that keeps every step of it.

Case Files SHIPPED · Apex Edge v0.6.8e

A conversation plus a file

Open a case and you open a chat with Navi that is the investigation — she answers first, in full, and offers to file what matters. Under the conversation stands the file: a set of registers that hold the case's substance, and the formed document that renders them as one readable story. The chat is where the thinking happens; the file is what the thinking leaves behind.

The registers

Leads

What might matter, not yet weighed. Everything enters here first — including every entry Navi logs, which lands as a lead until the operator promotes it.

Findings

What the evidence supports, stated as findings — the case's conclusions, earned and inspectable.

Entities

The people, organizations and machines the case touches — the cast, kept straight.

Observables

The concrete things seen: addresses, hashes, domains, names — the facts a watch or a search can match against.

Tasks

The work queue of the case — what Navi proposes, what the operator sets, what stands done.

Evidence

Exhibits with custody: what it is, where it came from, its hash — the chain kept on the face of the record.

Timeline

The story in order — events as the case establishes them, so the narrative never has to be reconstructed from memory.

Filing that knows its destination

Filing is destination-aware. When Navi offers to file a reply, she names the part of the case the content belongs to — this is a lead, that is an observable — and files it there when the operator says the word; the receipt names where it landed, and names anything that was left out. Her own logged entries ride in as leads and stay leads until the operator promotes them: the machine proposes, the detective disposes.

The formed document

The registers form one document — Cases/<Type>/<Name>.md — kept in the Vault and rendered reading-first: the case as a written file, leads to timeline, readable end to end. The case brief draws on it: ask for the brief and Navi lays out where the case stands — what is known, what is only led, what is still open — from the record, not from vibes.

Born briefed, shelf attached

A case born from a Wire story arrives with its first lead cited and a brief already written. Navi stages the follow-up work as proposed tasks — Approve or Dismiss, the operator's call on each. And every case carries a reference shelf, collapsible so it never crowds the work: the place Wire stories and Pulse hits collect, cited and attached, feeding the registers as the case grows.

Gone means gone

Delete a case and its formed document in the Vault is removed with it — gone means gone. What the case keeps is a single custody line: the record that the case existed and was closed out, and nothing of its substance. Deletion in Apex is an act with a meaning, not a rug things get swept under.

ON THE HORIZON — CASE-BORN CHIPS

Tools that graduate

Nothing in this section ships today. Investigations are proving grounds: a tool hacked together mid-case — a one-off script that cracked the evidence open — is a chip waiting to be born. The pipeline on paper: the case file is the chip's birth certificate, the tool is hardened into a cartridge, and it ships under the trust wall every chip ships under — provenance on the cartridge, install an operator act. The first named example is a cryptocurrency investigation suite. The cases are real today; their graduates are coming.
