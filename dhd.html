DHD.

The operator's pocket door — their own Apex, from their phone, admitted one device at a time and revocable the same way. Part of the danger, the good kind: access to her anywhere.

DHD SHIPPED · Apex Edge v0.6.8e

A door per device

DHD does not run on a single static passcode shared by everything the operator owns. Each device is approved on its own — admitted as itself, listed among the seat's trusted devices — and each device can be cut off on its own, without disturbing the others. Lose a phone and you revoke a device, not the house: the door that phone carried closes, every other door stands. Admission is per device, named, and revocable, because a door you cannot close is not a door.

The whole suite, from the phone

The phone is not a remote control with three buttons. Through DHD, the full Apex suite is manageable from the phone alone — chat, cases, the Wire, the day brief — in an interface designed for the phone's width rather than a desktop squeezed onto it. The SIEM line included: Seraph's per-sensor dashboards are read and worked from DHD, which was an acceptance criterion for the line, benched at a phone's width before it shipped.

SIEM — manageable from this door

Alerts in her voice

When something needs the operator — a detection, a knock, a lead awaiting review — the alert that reaches the phone is Navi's own voice, delivered by Web Push: she announces it in her words, in chat and on the lock screen, the way she announces everything. The bell keeps the record; her word is the tap on the shoulder.

Stepped-up acts, audited

Reading is one thing; managing is another. Management acts taken from the phone — the consequential kind — step up through the Operator Badge before they execute, and every one of them lands in an audit trail: what was done, from which device, when. The pocket door is powerful because it is accountable, not in spite of it.

The door is the operator's own

One thing this page will never do: say where anybody's door is. A DHD door belongs to its operator — its address is theirs, its devices are theirs, its hours are theirs. What can be described in public is the shape of the thing, and the shape is this: a per-device, revocable, audited door between an operator and their own Navi, carried in a pocket, working from anywhere the road reaches.
