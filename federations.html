FEDERATIONS — NAMED COUNTRIES ON THE MESH

One road. Many flags.

People keep using the two words as if they were one. They are not. The Mesh is how Apex machines reach each other. A Federation is a named group of those machines, with a gate, a roster, and rules. This page is the difference — and the list of the countries so far.

In Apex itself you'll see federations called fedinets — the word the software uses. Same thing.

Mesh is not Federation

The Mesh — the roads

Pairwise, node to node. Two machines exchange cards, pin each other's fingerprints, and whole requests travel between them over signed doors. No names, no membership, no center. The Mesh is plumbing — good, honest, verified plumbing.

A Federation — a country

A named web of nodes under one flag. A gateway node admits newcomers and signs the roster — the list of who belongs. Membership is proven against that roster, not against a card. A gateway card gets you an introduction; the roster does the rest.

How a federation holds together

Trust is a local decision, everywhere. Joining a federation never hands anyone your machine. Each member sets their own lending terms — how much of each hour, how many generations, whether to lend while busy. The federation-wide shortcut exists (members can choose to trust the roster by default), and it is a choice each operator makes for themselves, guarded by caps, by their own work preempting any borrow, and by revocation that is instant and total. A stale roster degrades a node back to pairwise roads until it syncs again. Expulsion propagates at the next sync.

Strength shared. Ownership unchanged. That sentence is the whole constitution.

The Directory

Every federation listed here arrived the same way: a gateway card was submitted, its signature verified against the gateway it names, and its gate knocked on and answering. A listing is a pointer, not a promise — it proves the country is real and its door works. What its roster does with a knock is its own business.
