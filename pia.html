A sensor, as a product

PIA is a credit-card-sized box built on a Raspberry Pi 5 with 8 GB of memory. She ships with Apex preloaded in its sensor configuration and already registered with the Mesh — plug in network and power, and she takes her post. There is no setup wizard and nothing to configure on the box: the configuration a standard Apex seat reaches by hand, she arrives already holding.

What she does

Collection is passive. PIA reads the tables the segment already keeps — the ARP table and DHCP leases, the connection tables, the sign-in record in the auth log. No packet capture, and no payload hoarding: she reads who is present, who talks to whom, which doors are listening, and who signed in — never what anyone said. On that picture she runs Seraph's seven detections:

Watchlist

A flow or sign-in touches a watched term. Matching happens on the box, against the operator's list — the list never leaves the unit; only matches travel.

New face

A device the sensor has never inventoried appears on the segment.

Beaconing

A remote called on a metronome — regular intervals over a sustained span.

Impossible hours

A successful sign-in inside the quiet window the operator set.

Auth burst

A run of failed sign-ins from one source.

Scan

One source knocking on many of the sensor's ports at once — the pattern seen from the receiving end.

New listener

A known host grows a listening service that was not in its baseline.

Detections are reported to the operator's console over the Mesh, spoken in Navi's voice — the news travels; the data stays home. When the uplink is dark, the unit spools its reports on disk and drains them when the road returns, so a sensor on a bad link loses nothing. From the console, any detection can be filed as a case, born with its evidence hashed into custody.

One segment, honestly

The unit of sight is the segment: one sensor watches the segment it sits on — never a whole network from one box, and the dashboard says so. Every picture the unit sends carries its gaps, the sources that vantage could not read; a blind spot is stated, never papered over. More ground is not a wider claim. More ground is more sensors.

For small and mid-sized operators

A PIA sensor quick-connects to the Federation as a limited citizen. A client sensor reports its own network's picture up to its operator and gets nothing else: no sight of other clients, no borrow path, and per-client segregation in the console — one client's evidence never surfaces in another client's anything. The scope of the claim is kept honest: the box makes the watching continuous, around the clock. Judgment and response stay with people, under the operator's own pre-authorized policy.
