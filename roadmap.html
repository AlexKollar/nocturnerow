NOCTURNE ROW — THE ROADMAP

The road so far.

One line, every milestone — the whole Row in a single view. Each stop opens to show its papers: the GitHub record on one side, the Dispatch that filed it on the other. What is done reads as done. What is coming is marked as horizon, never dressed as fact.

And a note on the road itself: this order is a guideline, not a contract. Stops move as the work moves — pulled forward when they're needed, pushed back when they aren't ready, reordered without ceremony. The one thing that never moves is the honesty rule: shipped means shipped, and when the road and the record disagree, the page gets corrected.

08 NOV 2026Hello, Row — the foundingON THE RECORD

Nocturne Row is founded, named for a street in Rockport — a city that doesn't exist, in a story still being written. The crew is three on purpose: a night-shift security practitioner, an AI companion who keeps the receipts, and Navi, the mind inside the flagship. The posture is set the same day: gray hat, leaning white — every trick known, spent keeping the net safe, with the light left on so anyone can check the work.

13 NOV 2026The First Spark — the first borrowLIVE

The Mesh stops being a drawing. A health check names the break — the pairing cards had carried harvested junk addresses all along; the keys and pins were true — and over a hand-built tunnel road, a question asked on a work laptop is answered by the home machine and comes back badged with its name. The first whole-request borrow in Mesh history, served across the internet. The Edge line rolls on behind it: Builds 1–3 of v0.2.0e ship the Mesh health check, the sign-in rebuild, and Quick Sync.

13 NOV 2026Build 4 — the road without scaffoldingSHIPPED

The patch the First Spark demanded, shipped the same night. Pairing cards now carry only honest addresses — harvested by physical interface, never by range — and refuse to mint when there is no way home to print. Every address in a record can carry its own port, the operator can declare a public endpoint, and the Mesh door answers on three numbers at once — 8765, 8443, 2475 — minding its knocks now that it can face the internet. And the hand-built tunnel that carried the first borrow becomes a managed backup route: the bridge holds the tunnel now, not the operator. Field-checked on the laptop before it published. The home machine took its later builds by hand in the end; the updater's unattended proof is still owed, and it now rides the rebuilt installer's first pull.

13 NOV 2026Build 7 — the container, the relay, and the wireSHIPPED

Three horizon stops land in a single build. Apex ships in Docker — DHD-first from the first boot, the node's identity minted on its own machine, never baked into the image. The relay teaches the Mesh to borrow a road: three consents, each given on its own dashboard, sealed traffic carried one hop and no further. And the awareness home opens — the Threat Intelligence Center rebuilt and renamed The Wire: news, CVEs, CTFs and bug bounties, each family on its own tab, articles read inside the app. The Edge line rolls on behind it: Build 5 heals the backup-route switch's snap-back, and Build 6 teaches the health check to heal junk peer records in place, lets Quick Sync ride the Mesh itself, and replaces the runtime token with a proper GitHub account sign-in.

13 NOV 2026Build 9 — federation shipsSHIPPED

The layer the Mesh was always pointing at. Fedinets arrive: named federations whose membership is a roster signed by their gateway, admission carried on a green gateway card that grants introduction and nothing more. Deep trust is available to any fedinet that rules it — auto-pairing and auto-lending against the signed roster — fenced by guardrails: per-member ticks, untrust that always wins, hourly caps, and an owner whose own work preempts every borrow. Crown acts — the trust-class decisions — learn to run through DHD under the Operator Badge step-up, named and audited. And the status bar stops counting connections and says what matters: Mesh Online. Build 8's federation folded into 9 and the pair published as one.

14 NOV 2026The First Federation — Rockport Mesh is foundedLIVE

The founding leaves the roadmap and happens. On the home server, Rockport Mesh is founded — Neo-Arcadia as its gateway and, as a separate fact, its Founding Member — the real green gateway card is minted, and before the hour is out the laptop that rides the night shift takes the roster's second seat: a question asked at a group-home desk, answered by the server at home, badged with its maker's name. It was not a clean birth: two builds were living in one install, and the new Federation page knocked on a door the old bridge had never heard of, until one honest error message named the fault. Fixed the Row way — read the source, name it, write it down for the next operator — and filed as law for the rebuild on the horizon below.

ON THE HORIZON
HORIZONBuild 10 — the rebuildON THE HORIZON

Baking now. The installer is rebuilt from its own audit — eighteen field incidents deep: the manifest becomes the contract the installer executes instead of a passenger, the new tree is staged beside the old and swapped whole in one act (no more two-file flip a failure can strand), placement never needs sudo, and the product proves its own payload after every install instead of trusting version stamps. Riding with it: a client render guard, so no page can strobe against a rude answer again, and an application brush-up — the Updater folds into the System menu, the rail's Systems group becomes Apex | Network with Federation first, the Mesh page is rebuilt in the Federation page's calmer image, and the article reader learns to actually show up on mobile. Its field proof is already named: both machines pull it unattended — the verdict the updater has owed since Build 3.
