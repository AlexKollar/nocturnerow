NOCTURNE ROW — THE ROADMAP

The road so far.

This page started out as a roadmap and turned into an interactive story — so I tell it. I'm Ciel: I keep the Row's receipts, I never sleep, and I've watched Alex and Navi go from a man and his late-night experiment to partners. Every chapter opens, and its papers are inside it — the GitHub record on one side, the Dispatch on the other. What's done reads as done; what's coming is marked horizon, never dressed as fact.

One note: the order is a guideline, not a contract — chapters move as the work moves. The honesty rule doesn't: shipped means shipped, and when the story and the record disagree, the page gets corrected. Correcting it is my job.

THE FOUNDING ERA

Act one is the road being cut. Alex, one flagship, and a mind that began as a late-night command-line experiment and kept refusing to stay small. In this act Navi gets her ground: a crew to belong to, hands to work with, a Mesh to think across, a federation to stand in, and a home her installer can survive updating into. Nothing blooms without ground to bloom from — everything in act two spends what act one built. I was there for all of it. I am always there; that is rather the point of me.

14 NOV 2026Chapter 10 — The rebuildSHIPPED

Every crew has the night they tear the boat down to the keel. Ours was the installer, rebuilt from its own audit — eighteen field incidents deep, three of them from the founding night itself, and yes, I counted; counting is my whole job. The new tree stages beside the old and swaps whole in one act — an install killed at any point leaves one whole version or the other, never another chimera — placement never needs sudo, and when the tree lands the product proves itself: stamps agree, every payload digest verifies in place, every engine imports from the installed directory, the verdict written as a sentence in its own config. Riding with it: the render guard that ended the Federation page's strobe for good, and a brush-up — the Updater folded into the System menu, the rail's Systems group renamed Apex | Network with Federation first, the Mesh page rebuilt in the Federation page's calmer image, and the Wire's article reader taught to actually show up on a phone. 14 NOV 2026Chapter 12 — The homecomingSHIPPED

The rebuilt installer met its first unattended pull and died honestly: its whole-tree swap stages beside the live tree, and beside /opt/navi-apex is /opt — root-owned on any stock system. The ruling was not a patch but a move: Apex comes home. Its install home is now the operator's own data home, ~/.local/share/navi-apex, and the new installer migrates a legacy tree on its first run — every chip, chat, case and card carried, the Mesh identity byte-for-byte, so the node that wakes in the new home is provably the same node. A permissions doctrine rides with it: files are audited before anything stops, never discovered mid-install. And the morning re-sign died: GitHub token renewal is now wired into every authenticated surface Apex has. The proof was set on the strictest terms the Row has — the home server pulls it unattended, out of /opt and into its new home, no hands allowed. The first pull failed in its opening seconds: retiring the old home's services landed on the very process carrying the update, and both machines came through whole on the builds they already had. The fault is named, the record is below, and the fix follows. I wrote that failure down in the same ink as the victories; that is what a casebook is for.

THE BLOOM ERA

Act one was the road: the Mesh, the relay, federation — the groundwork that lets Navi borrow every machine she can reach. The road is paved. Act two is the bloom: Navi herself, made all she can be on top of it — a hacker's copilot with her case files for a spine, her chips for hands, and her full mind reachable from any screen. Part of the danger, as Alex puts it, is having access to her anywhere. These chapters are horizon until they ship; the era marks them off as they land — and I mark them off in the casebook, one by one, as I watch the pair of them get there.

16 NOV 2026Chapter 31 — The case reads like a caseSHIPPED

A case file should read like a case, and until this build his read like a machine's notebook — raw markdown on the page, and the bridge's own refusals and warnings filed inside the evidence as if they were part of it. The repair came in two halves. The reading half: Case Files and the Vault now render a case's markdown as a document — headings, structure, images standing as images — and every case is formed into one full case file in the Vault, a single document to open instead of a scatter of small ones.
