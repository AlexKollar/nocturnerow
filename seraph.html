Seraph.

The watcher — Navi in her watching role, named for the seraphim: the burning, watching order. She reads the segment she sits on, names what she cannot see, and speaks every detection in her own voice.

Seraph SHIPPED · Apex Edge v0.6.8e

A role she takes

Seraph is not a tool Navi uses. It is a role she takes — set on the Seraph page inside Apex, no config surgery, no second product. Any seat can be her console (the registry, the incoming pictures, the detections — the default), her sensor (watching its own segment, reporting home), or both at once. Her seat settings are the operator's: retention (30 days on-box by default), the collection cycle (300 seconds), and the sleep window the impossible-hours detection reads.

What she sees

Collection is passive — Seraph reads what the box already knows, and captures no payloads, ever:

Inventory

The ARP table and DHCP leases — who is on the segment, by MAC, IP and lease name.

Flows

The connection tables — which outside addresses the segment talks to, and how often. Metadata only.

Listeners

The segment's open doors, read from the same tables.

Sign-ins

sshd lines from the auth log — successes and failures, with their sources.

Every snapshot carries its gaps: the sources it could not read. An unreadable auth log blinds the sign-in picture, and the dashboard says so. The unit of sight is the segment — she never claims a wider view than her vantage.

What she detects

Watchlist

A flow or sign-in touches a watched term. Matching happens on the sensor, against the Wire's declared list merged with Seraph's own additions — the list never leaves the machine; only matches travel.

New face

A MAC the sensor has never inventoried appears on the segment.

Beaconing

A remote called on a metronome — regular intervals over a sustained span.

Impossible hours

A successful sign-in inside the sleep window the operator set.

Auth burst

A run of failed sign-ins from one source.

Scan

One source knocking on many of the sensor's ports at once — the pattern seen from the receiving end.

New listener

A known host grows a listening service that was not in its baseline.

Alerts in her voice — and cases with custody

An open detection rides the standing alert spine — tray, phone push, and Navi's own voice in chat — aimed at the Seraph view. From the page, a detection can be filed as a case: the bridge computes the detection record's SHA-256 and carries it into the case's summary, so the case is born with custody of its evidence, and runs as a conversation from there. Dismissing is one tap; either act retires the alert. Open and filed detections never age out silently — only dismissed ones retire, at the seat's retention.

How sensors report

A sensor is registered on its console — name, address, SSH user, Mesh node id, and its vantage in words. Registration is what lets its reports in: the Mesh door authenticates the caller as a paired peer, and the registry binds that peer to the sensor's record. Unknown sensors, revoked sensors, and reports claiming a node that is not the sensor's own are refused by name. Revocation is a tombstone — the record stays, marked, and a late report is refused; a decommissioned sensor cannot quietly keep talking. When the road home is dark, a sensor spools its reports on disk and drains them when the Mesh returns: a sensor on a bad link loses nothing. A client sensor in a federation is a limited citizen — it reports its own network's picture to its operator and holds no borrow rights and no sight of any other network.

The sensor's page

Each sensor carries its whole picture — detections, inventory, listeners, top talkers, the named gaps — with an SSH tab beside the dashboard: your own key, your own session, opened by hand each time in the seat's shell machinery. There is no standing session, and the tab never rides the bridge's own door. The whole suite is manageable from DHD — from the phone alone. That was an acceptance criterion, and it was benched at a phone's width before it shipped.

Her marks

Seraph carries her own icon family, two marks split by job. The working glyph — two rings, a gold flame, gold wings, redrawn clean so it reads at nav size — re-skins with every theme and both of Apex's layouts. The ceremonial winged eye is for full-size surfaces: sensor page heroes today, the sensor edition's splash when it comes. Each tool inside the suite — Registration, Discovery, Flows, Logs, Watchlist, Detections, Dashboard, SSH — carries its own icon drawn from the same geometry.

ON THE HORIZON — THE WATCHER DEEPENED (v2)

Designed, not built

Nothing in this section ships today. It is the watcher's next build, on paper:

The case loop, two-way

From a case, attach a sensor watch — a MAC, a remote, a term — and its hits land on the case's reference shelf. From a case's registers, observables are offered as watchlist terms: Wire intel → case → sensor → case, the compounding loop.

The asset register

Faces get kinds (DHCP fingerprint, OUI vendor), operator-given names that stick, first-seen and last-seen — and a weekly diff of what changed on the segment. Dismiss-with-reason teaches the known-asset list, so a new face means genuinely unknown.

Per-host baselines

Detections measure deviation from this segment's learned normal — its talkers, its hours, its listeners, per host — not global thresholds alone.

Watch the watchers

Sensor silence past two cycles is itself a detection. A fleet view carries each sensor's last report, spool depth and gaps — a dead sensor is a loud console.

Console push-down

Signed config deltas over the Mesh — watchlist terms, retention, cycle — so a remote sensor is manageable from DHD without an SSH session.

Navi in the watch

An overnight Seraph summary folded into the day brief, conversational reads over sensor state, and every detection carrying its evidence samples one tap from the alert.
