SIEM.

The security line inside Apex, in two halves. Seraph watches the wire. Throne is where what she sees gets judged.

Seraph SHIPPED · v0.6.8e · Throne ON THE HORIZON · v3

Not a separate product

The SIEM line is a configuration of Apex, not a fork — no second install, no second product to buy. Any Apex seat can take the work up from inside the app, where a top-level SIEM group holds the line: Seraph stands there today, and Throne takes the seat beside her when the line's third build lands. The sensor edition of Apex — the shape PIA ships in — is this same system with the configuration preloaded.

Navi becomes Seraph

In this line, Navi is Seraph. The watcher is not a tool she uses; it is a role she takes — the way the whole system is her, carried into the watching. And Throne is her seat: the console where she sits beside her human operator and does the work, judgment and response as a partnership rather than an automation that replaces him. The line's sentence is short: Seraph watches; the Throne judges.

The two halves

Seraph — the watcher

Passive sight over the segment she sits on: the living inventory, who talks to whom, the listening doors, the sign-in record — and seven detections spoken in Navi's own voice, any one of them fileable as a case with its evidence hashed into custody.

Throne — the SOC

Roles — any seat, three shapes

Console

Holds the sensor registry, receives the sensors' pictures, shows the detections. The default — and the seat Throne will be taken from.

Sensor

Watches its own segment and reports home to its console over the Mesh — spooling through a dark road and draining when it lights.

Both

A seat that watches and consoles at once. The common single-box shape: one machine, the whole floor.

The segment doctrine

A sensor sees the one segment it is plugged into — never a whole network from one box, and it says so. Every snapshot names its gaps, the sources that vantage could not read, right on the dashboard: a blind spot is stated, never papered over. More ground is not a wider claim; more ground is more sensors.

The shape for small business

A PIA sensor arrives pre-registered, plugs into a small business's network, and quick-connects to the Federation — around-the-clock watching in a credit-card-sized box, for the class of business that could never staff a SOC. The honesty is in the shape of the claim: the box makes the watching continuous. Response stays a human act — today by the operator's hand, and under Throne by the operator's own pre-set policy. Client sensors are limited citizens of the federation: a client box reports its own network's picture to its operator and holds no sight of any other network, and per-client evidence never surfaces in another client's anything.
